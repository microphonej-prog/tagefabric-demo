Where a sports bra fabric fails

A bra fails differently from a legging. The underband loses tension first, then the cup edge curls, then the whole garment rides up during a run. All three trace back to the knit: modulus, recovery and how the fabric behaves when it is cut on the bias for a curved cup.

Support is a modulus question

Support is not the same as thickness. A 250 gsm high-modulus nylon spandex holds the underband with less fabric than a soft 220 gsm knit that feels substantial in the hand but relaxes after ten wears. Ask for elongation and residual growth, then test the underband after repeated extension — that number is the bra.

Bonded versus cut-and-sew

Bonded cups and laser-cut edges need a thin, dense, smooth fabric that takes adhesive without strike-through or bubbling. Ultrafine 15D/20D nylon is the quality most brands land on. Cut-and-sew bras tolerate heavier, more forgiving knits and are cheaper to develop while you are still fitting the pattern.

Sweat and print

High-sweat categories — spin, HIIT, hot yoga — want a moisture-wicking face rather than a brushed one, because a peached surface holds water against the skin. For printed bras, polyester interlock takes sublimation with sharper detail and less colour loss than nylon.

Buyer questions

What fabric weight suits a sports bra?

170–250 gsm. Light support and bralettes sit at 170–200 gsm; medium and high support want 220–250 gsm on a high-modulus yarn so the underband keeps tension without bulk.

Is nylon or polyester better for sports bras?

Nylon gives a softer, cooler hand and better recovery for support layers. Polyester wins on print detail and price for printed or high-volume ranges. Many brands run nylon for the bra and polyester for the matching printed range.

Can you supply fabric for bonded cups?

Yes — ultrafine 15D/20D nylon in a smooth, dense build is what bonded cup lines usually specify. Tell us the adhesive process you run and we will confirm the face finish that suits it.
