Bib shorts fail at the inner thigh, not the print

The first thing to go on a bib short is the inner-thigh panel: pilling, then shine, then a hole where the saddle has abraded the knit through. Behind that comes the leg gripper losing its hold and the short riding up. Both are knit-density and recovery problems, and both are decided at the fabric stage.

Abrasion and density

Abrasion resistance per filament improves with density and with a tighter, flatter knit surface. Our cycling compression quality is built dense rather than thick, because a heavier fabric that pills will still fail; a denser one that keeps its surface will not. Ask for Martindale results on the actual panel colour — dark dyes on the same knit do not always test the same.

Compression gradient

Compression has to be consistent along the leg, not just high at the gripper. A fabric with high modulus and predictable recovery lets you design a graduated fit; a soft knit forces you to compensate with elastic, which is where comfort complaints come from on long rides.

Jerseys and panels

Summer jerseys want a light, fast-wicking knit — not a cotton-feel one. Mesh belongs under the jersey straps, at the side panels and on the back where airflow matters more than opacity. Keep in mind that mesh is a structural choice: it should be specified where the garment can tolerate transparency.

Buyer questions

What fabric is used for bib shorts?

A dense, high-modulus knit with a flat surface, usually nylon spandex at 220–260 gsm. The panel that matters is the inner thigh: it needs abrasion resistance and opacity together, which is why a soft studio knit is the wrong choice even when it feels better in the hand.

How do you test abrasion?

Martindale (ISO 12947) or equivalent on the finished panel colour, before bulk. We can run the test on the sample and send the result with the swatch pack.

Do you supply the chamois?

No — we supply the shell fabric. The pad, the gripper elastic and the bib mesh are separate components we can advise on but do not make.

Send the bib pattern and the panel list

We will tell you which quality fits each panel, and where a cheaper knit will do the job without failing at the inner thigh in month three.
