Is 4-way stretch always better than 2-way for yoga wear?

Performance & testing · TAGE FABRIC technical note

No. Four-way stretch means the fabric extends in both warp/vertical and weft/horizontal directions; two-way means it extends mainly across the width. For leggings and sports bras, four-way is the right default because the garment is loaded in both directions when you squat, hinge and rotate.

Two-way stretch becomes the better choice in three situations. First, structured pieces: Ponte Roma and rib knits rely on low lengthwise stretch to hold a flare, a waistband or a rib line — a high vertical stretch collapses the silhouette. Second, cut-and-sew tops and jackets where you want drape rather than cling. Third, cost: a two-way knit is often cheaper per meter at the same weight.

The number that actually predicts garment failure is not the direction but the recovery — how much of the elongation comes back after cycling the fabric. Ask for elongation and recovery separately, in both directions, and ask at what load. A fabric quoting “180% stretch” says nothing about whether the knee will bag after twenty wears.

For yoga, a practical minimum is 120–150% horizontal elongation with recovery above 90%, and vertical elongation matched to the pattern — 60–80% is plenty for a legging, and much less for a Ponte. Test on a garment, not on a swatch: sew a leg, wear it, squat, and look at the knee and the waistband.
