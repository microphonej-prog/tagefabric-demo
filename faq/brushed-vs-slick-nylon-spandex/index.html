Brushed (peached) versus slick nylon spandex — what actually differs beyond hand-feel?

Fabric science · TAGE FABRIC technical note

Both start as the same circular-knit nylon spandex. The difference comes from a brushing or peaching pass that raises the surface fibers, which changes four things that matter to a garment program.

First, hand-feel: brushing removes the cold, technical touch that makes cheap activewear feel like swimwear, and it is the single biggest reason the “buttery” legging category exists. Second, light reflection: a brushed face reads matte, so garment seams and body contours show less than on a slick face.

Third, durability: raised fibers are the raw material of pilling. A brushed fabric needs a tighter brushing pass and a pilling test (ISO 12945-2) before bulk, and it will still pill faster at high-friction points than a slick knit. Fourth, print and bonding: sublimation and heat-transfer sit better on a smooth surface, and bonded seams need a clean, low-lint face.

Specify it explicitly. “Brushed” on a tech pack is not enough — state the pilling grade you will accept (3–4 at 2,000 rubs is a common bar), whether the face is one-side or two-side brushed, and which side faces the body. On our nude-feel quality we specify a single brushing pass and test before bulk; on compression qualities we keep the slick face, because compression garments punish fuzzed fibers.
